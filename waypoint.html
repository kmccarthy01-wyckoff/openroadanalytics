The Visibility Read
+

The Visibility Read is a single number from 0 to 100 that estimates how visible your brand is when real buyers ask AI assistants about your category. It is a directional read generated in seconds from live AI responses — not the analyst-verified Waypoint Score™ that comes with the full Audit.

A score of 61 means: when someone asks Claude or ChatGPT about fast casual restaurants in Austin, Taco Tornado appears in roughly 6 out of 10 relevant AI responses — and when it does appear, it's usually mentioned positively and relatively early in the answer.

A score of 44 means: Burrito Rocket appears in fewer than half of relevant AI responses, and when it does appear, it's often mentioned with less confidence or later in the response than its competitor.

75–100AI consistently recommends your brand with confidence. Strong citation density and positive context.
50–74AI knows your brand and mentions it regularly, but not always first or with full confidence.
25–49AI has limited signals about your brand. Appears inconsistently, often with caveats.
0–24AI rarely or never mentions your brand in relevant searches. Effectively invisible.

The Visibility Read is the average of three dimension scores: Discover, Verify, and Recommend, each equally weighted. The full Waypoint Audit scores these same three dimensions against measured evidence — your actual review volume, directory listings, and press coverage — rather than inferring them from AI responses.

// Section 02
How the Scan Works
+

When you run a Waypoint scan, here is exactly what happens:

01
Claude generates 9 buyer queries — real questions a person would actually type into an AI assistant when researching your category. Some are unbranded category searches. Some mention your brand directly. All are written the way a real buyer searches, not the way a marketer describes their product.
02
All 9 queries are fired at two AI engines simultaneously — Claude (Anthropic) and GPT-4o (OpenAI). That produces 18 real AI responses, each answered independently.
03
Each response is analyzed for three things: Was your brand mentioned? Where in the response (first or buried)? Was the context positive, neutral, or negative?
04
Scores are calculated per dimension and averaged across both engines. The three dimension scores are averaged to produce your final Visibility Read.
Example query generated for Taco Tornado scan:
"Where should I go for tacos in Austin — any recommendations?"
Claude's actual response (paraphrased):
"Austin has a great taco scene. Taco Tornado on South Congress is a popular choice — known for their breakfast tacos and late-night hours. Burrito Rocket is another option but draws more of a lunch crowd..."
What that response scores:
Taco Tornado: mentioned first → position score 3 · positive context ("popular") → sentiment score 3 · no third-party citation → bonus 0 · RAW = 3 × 3 + 0 = 9
Burrito Rocket: mentioned second → position score 2 · neutral context → sentiment score 2 · no third-party citation → bonus 0 · RAW = 2 × 2 + 0 = 4
// Section 03
Discover — What It Measures
+

Discover measures whether AI surfaces your brand when buyers search your category. AI engines cite brands whose content vocabulary mirrors the queries buyers type. If buyers ask "best breakfast tacos Austin" and your website talks about "artisanal morning cuisine," AI can't connect the two.

Taco Tornado68
Burrito Rocket41
"Taco Tornado's website opens with the exact phrases buyers use — 'best tacos in Austin,' 'late night food Austin,' 'South Congress eats.' Burrito Rocket leads with brand story. AI engines surface Taco Tornado more consistently in category searches because the language matches."

The 3 prompts used for this dimension are unbranded category searches — the kind a buyer types before they know which brand they want. Your score here tells you whether AI organically surfaces your brand when someone searches your category.

// Section 04
Verify — What It Measures
+

Verify measures how much independent third-party evidence AI engines have to back up a mention of your brand. AI engines don't just repeat your website — they weight external sources like review platforms, press coverage, directories, and industry publications far more heavily than anything you publish yourself.

Taco Tornado59
Burrito Rocket38
"Taco Tornado appears in Yelp, Austin Chronicle, Eater Austin, and TripAdvisor — sources AI engines treat as independent validation. Burrito Rocket's coverage is thinner. When AI says 'Taco Tornado is popular,' it has evidence. When it mentions Burrito Rocket, it hedges."

The 3 prompts used here are brand-specific reputation queries — "is [brand] any good," "reviews for [brand]," "[brand] vs [competitor]." Your score here tells you whether AI can confidently vouch for you or has to qualify every mention.

// Section 05
Recommend — What It Measures
+

Recommend measures whether your content is structured so AI engines can actually extract and cite it. AI engines don't browse — they extract. Content that is formatted as direct answers to specific questions gets cited. Content that requires navigation, interpretation, or reading between the lines gets ignored.

Taco Tornado56
Burrito Rocket53
"Both brands are close here. Taco Tornado has a FAQ page with hours, pricing, and menu highlights in extractable format. Burrito Rocket's equivalent information is buried in a PDF menu. Narrow gap — but Taco Tornado's structured data gives it a consistent citation edge."

The 3 prompts here are direct information queries — "tell me about [brand]," "what makes [brand] different," "overview of [brand] and [competitor]." Your score here tells you whether AI can answer specific questions about you or has to say "I don't have enough information."

// Section 06
The Scoring Math — Exactly How Numbers Are Calculated
+

For every AI response, each brand gets a raw score calculated like this:

Position Score (1–3)Mentioned first or only = 3 · Mentioned within 150 chars of competitor = 2 · Mentioned after competitor = 1 · Not mentioned = 0
Sentiment Score (1–3)More positive words nearby = 3 · Neutral = 2 · More negative words nearby = 1
Third-Party BonusResponse cites an external source = +1 · No external citation = 0
Raw ScorePosition × Sentiment + Bonus · Maximum possible = 10

Using the Taco Tornado example above — mentioned first (3), positive context (3), no third-party bonus (0):

3 × 3 + 0 = 9 out of 10

After scoring all 6 responses in a dimension:

AverageAdd up all 6 raw scores ÷ 6
Normalize(Average ÷ 10) × 100 = dimension score out of 100
Final Score(Discover + Verify + Recommend) ÷ 3
Taco Tornado: (68 + 59 + 56) ÷ 3 = 61
Burrito Rocket: (41 + 38 + 53) ÷ 3 = 44

The Primary Gap Diagnostic
+

After scoring, Waypoint identifies your single most impactful gap — the one dimension where improvement will move your score the most. It then gives you three specific actions to address it.

The diagnostic looks at your three dimension scores and finds the weakest one. That becomes your primary gap. The actions are sequenced by impact — the highest-ROI change first.

For Burrito Rocket, the weakest dimension is Verify (38). The primary gap diagnosis: "AI engines lack the third-party signals to recommend you with confidence." The first action: complete all directory profiles and request reviews — because one verified Yelp review carries more AI weight than any website update.

The baseline date stamps when the scan ran. Re-run in 30 days to measure improvement.

The Visibility Read is a directional estimate derived from live AI engine responses at the time of the scan and may vary on re-scan as AI model behavior evolves. The analyst-verified Waypoint Score™, delivered with the full Waypoint Audit, additionally measures review, directory, and press signals against a documented scoring rubric.
